How this works

You are building the one-page diagram the assignment asks for, and saving it as a PDF to hand in on Canvas.

  1. Pick your biome. Choosing one fills in starting values for dominant vegetation, precipitation and temperature. Edit them freely — you should be able to explain whatever you leave there.
  2. Add the pieces. Drag one in from the palette, or just click it. Every group ends in a dashed Custom… piece for anything the palette does not have, and you can double-click any piece to rename it — a desert's Producers might read saguaro & creosote.
  3. Draw the arrows. Switch to ➡ Connect and drag from one piece to another. There are three kinds:
    • + amplifies — more of this, more of that.
    • − dampens — more of this, less of that.
    • ⚡ energy flows to — the food chain: who eats whom. Use these to run producers up to your apex predators.
    Click an arrow you have already drawn to cycle it through the three; ✂ Remove deletes pieces and arrows.
  4. Watch the panel on the right. It counts your three systems, finds the feedback loops, and ticks off the assignment checklist as you work. Energy arrows are left out of the loop count on purpose — a food chain is a chain, not a feedback loop.
  5. ⚡ Shock the system. Click a piece, then shock it, to see whether a small change runs away, keeps circulating, or fades out.
  6. ✎ Write-up. Your sustainability lens (one challenge or opportunity, why it matters, what you would do), your explanation of the diagram, and a note crediting which specific parts were AI-assisted.
  7. ⤓ Save as PDF and submit that file. This is a collaborative effort, but every student hands in their own copy.

Not sure where to start? Load the worked example Coral reef & fishing town — a full food chain wired into a fishing town, with two feedback loops pulling against each other.
